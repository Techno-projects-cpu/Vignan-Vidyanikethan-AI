This is a language model writing to you. It can be confident and wrong at the same time. The citations are the part you can check, and checking them is the point.

Last updated 27 October 2026

01 / What an answer is

An answer is generated text produced by a machine, from a question plus whatever context the app could assemble — your notes, the chapter you have open, or a page the app fetched. It is not a teacher, not an examiner and not a source. It has no memory of what your syllabus actually requires, and it cannot know what your teacher said in class.

02 / How it can be wrong

It can invent a fact, a date, a formula, a person or a citation. It can be right about a method and wrong about the arithmetic. It can follow an instruction in a quoted page that you did not intend. It can be out of date — its training has a cut-off, and the textbook in front of you may differ. A confident tone is not evidence: the model is built to sound fluent, not to be sure.

03 / What to do before you rely on it

Open the citation. Every cited line points at the page it came from, and that page is real. Recompute the arithmetic yourself. Compare a definition with your textbook. If the app says something and your book says something else, assume the book is right until you can show otherwise. If there is no citation, treat the sentence as a hint about where to look rather than as an answer.

04 / Not professional advice

Nothing here is medical, legal, financial or psychological advice. It is not a diagnosis, a legal opinion, an investment recommendation or a crisis service. If you are unwell, in danger, or being harmed, talk to a trusted adult, your school, or the emergency services in your area — not to this app. Study help is the whole of what this project offers.

05 / Exam and assessment rules

Whether you may use an AI assistant at all during an assessment is set by your school, your teacher and the examination board, not by this page. The acceptable use policy requires the work you hand in to be yours. When in doubt, ask before the exam, not after.

06 / Reporting a bad answer

If an answer is wrong, unsafe or cites a page that does not support it, tell your teacher and send the report to the project operator with the question and the answer. Wrong answers are the most useful bug reports this project gets, and a repeated failure is used to change the prompts, the guardrails or the fallback source rather than being explained away.
