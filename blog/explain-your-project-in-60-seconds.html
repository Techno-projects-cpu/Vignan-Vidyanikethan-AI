How to explain a school project in sixty seconds

A judge decides whether to keep listening in about a minute. Five sentences, in a fixed order, survive that minute.

Published 7 November 20264 min readBy a student, for students

01Sentence one: the problem, in your own words

Not the technology. The problem. In my school, students study from a chapter and the doubts come at night, when the teacher is not there and the internet may not be working. One sentence, no adjectives, and it must be true of a person you can name — you, a classmate, your cousin in another district.

If the first sentence is about how impressive the stack is, the judge hears a project. If it is about a person who has a problem, the judge hears a reason for the project to exist. That is the whole difference in the first ten seconds.

02Sentences two and three: what you built

Two sentences, and they must fit together: what it does for that person, then how. So I built a study space that answers only from the pages a student already has, and shows the book and page under every answer. When the pages do not cover the question, it says so instead of inventing something.

Notice what is not in there: the model names, the framework, the number of files. All of that is true and none of it belongs in the first minute. A judge who is interested will ask; a judge who is lost will not.

Nobody has ever asked a follow-up question because the first sentence mentioned a framework.

03Sentence four: how they can check it themselves

This is the sentence almost every school project leaves out, and it is the one that changes the conversation. Give the judge a test they can run in the room, with their own hands, not a demonstration they have to trust.

Open it on your phone, add any page — even a page you type yourself — ask something only that page answers, and read the line under the answer. If the page and the sentence are not there, that is a bug and I want to hear about it.

A claim the judge can break is more convincing than a claim repeated louder. It also gives them a reason to be involved rather than entertained.

04Sentence five: what it cannot do

Say the limits before they are found. It does not have permission to include any published textbook, so the shelf is written by me and students add their own books. The web search is labelled separately and not verified. There has been no security audit beyond what the repository shows.

This feels like handing over marks. It is the opposite. A student who knows exactly where the edges are is a student who has built the thing and not just presented it — and the first question that would have damaged you has already been answered, in your own words, calmly.

05Then stop talking

Sixty seconds is about one hundred and fifty words. Practise with a timer, in front of a person, until you can do it without reading. Then do the harder exercise: do it in forty seconds, without any of the numbers, and see whether the five sentences still stand.

Have one page in your hand that a judge can keep: the test in three steps, and one honest limit. Not a brochure — a page they can use when you are not standing there. Everything else on the table is for the questions that follow.

06When you do not know the answer

You will be asked something you have not thought about. The answer that works is short: I don't know. Here is how I would find out, and here is what I would check first. Then say the first check out loud. Judges are not testing whether a school student knows everything; they are testing whether the student can think past the edge of what they prepared.

Two things not to do: invent a number, and say "it's AI" as though that explained anything. The first is a lie that will be caught, and the second hands the credit for your work to a service that did not do it.

If the project is a study tool, as this one is, the best thing you can do at the end is hand the judge the page that shows how to check it, and let them work the phone. The demonstration that sticks is one they drove themselves.

How you know it worked

You will know it worked when the judge's next question is about what you built, instead of asking what it is.
