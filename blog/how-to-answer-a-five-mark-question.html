How to answer a five-mark question

Five marks is not five sentences. It is five things the examiner is looking for, and they are almost always the same five.

Published 7 November 20264 min readBy a student, for students

01Count the marks before you write

In a school paper, marks are a list of things the examiner is checking. A two-mark question wants a definition and an example. A three-mark question wants a definition, a mechanism and a consequence. A five-mark question wants a definition, a labelled diagram or equation, a mechanism, an example with numbers, and a limitation.

So before writing anything, count: five marks, five things. Write the five headings in the margin as a list, then fill them in. This is not padding; it is the answer. A candidate who writes one long correct paragraph for five marks usually earns three, because two of the things were never mentioned.

02The shape that earns all five

The shape below works in almost every subject, with small changes of wording.

  1. The definition, in the school's words. One or two sentences, using the term the question used. Do not paraphrase the definition into something prettier; the examiner is looking for the phrasing in the book.
  2. The relation or the diagram. An equation with every symbol defined, or a labelled diagram with three or four labels and a direction. Marks are given per label, so labels are the cheapest marks on the paper.
  3. How it happens. Three or four sentences of mechanism, in order, each one following the last. "Because" is the word that earns this section.
  4. An example, with numbers or a named case. A solved value, a named process, a familiar device. A number in the right unit proves you can use the idea, not just state it.
  5. What it does not cover. One line: the condition it needs, the case where it fails, the assumption it makes. This is the line that separates five from three, and almost nobody writes it.

03Write in sentences a marker can tick

Examiners mark quickly and with a scheme. Help them do it:

  • One idea per sentence. A sentence carrying two ideas can only be ticked once.
  • Name the thing instead of saying "it". The current increases is tickable; it increases is not.
  • Underline the technical term the first time you use it. It is not decoration; it is a signpost.
  • Put the equation on its own line, and define the symbols directly under it.
  • Never write "as we know that" or "basically". Both cost you time and earn nothing.

If you run out of space, the margin is part of your answer. A labelled diagram in the margin is worth more than a conclusion paragraph.

04Time it, or lose the last question

A five-mark question is worth roughly five minutes in a three-hour paper, and most students spend twelve on the first one. Put a time next to the question number when you start it, and move on when the time is up even if the answer is unfinished. An unfinished five-mark answer with four of the five things written earns four. The question you never reached earns nothing.

Leave the gaps you skipped visibly numbered at the top of the page, so you can come back in the last ten minutes. Most papers have one question everyone misjudged; being the student who returned to it is worth more than being the student who wrote neatly.

05Practise the shape, not the content

Take three five-mark questions from old class tests — not for the answers, for the shape. Write only the five headings in the margin, for all three, in ten minutes. No paragraphs. Then check against the mark scheme or your teacher's notes: did your five headings match the five things being marked?

Do that once a week and the shape becomes automatic, which is what you want, because in the exam the hard part should be the physics and not the layout. If you would rather have questions to practise on that come from your own pages, the quiz screen in the app writes them from the chapter you added and keeps the page number, so you can check the mark scheme against the book.

How you know it worked

You will know it worked when your answer has five tickable things in it, and you can name all five before you start writing.
