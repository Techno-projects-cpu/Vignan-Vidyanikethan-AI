How to revise a chapter you have already forgotten

Reading it again feels like revision and usually is not. Here is the version that hurts a little and stays.

Published 7 November 20264 min readBy a student, for students

01Start with a blank page, not the book

Open the notebook, write the chapter's name at the top, and give yourself five minutes to write everything you can still remember: definitions, formulas, the diagram, the example the teacher solved. Do not stop to look anything up. Ugly is fine. Wrong is fine. Empty is information.

This is uncomfortable for exactly the reason it works. The moment you try to produce something, your mind finds the edge of what it actually knows. Reading the chapter does the opposite: every sentence feels familiar, so you finish the page believing you have revised, when all you have done is recognise words you saw before.

Recognition is not recall. Only one of them shows up in the exam hall.

02Sort what you wrote into three piles

Now open the book, and go through the chapter section by section. Take a pencil and mark your own page three ways:

  • A mark — you wrote it, and it matches the book. Leave it alone.
  • B mark — you wrote something, but it is incomplete, half-remembered or in the wrong order.
  • C mark — you did not write it at all, and the book says it is important.

This takes about ten minutes for a chapter and it replaces an hour of reading, because it tells you what to read. Most students who "revised the whole chapter" spend their time on the A items, which are the ones that needed nothing.

03Read only the third pile

Read the C items properly — the paragraph, the worked example, the labels on the diagram — and rewrite each one in your own words in the notebook, one short line each. Not a copy. A line you could say to someone.

Then go back to the B items and fix only what was wrong. If a formula came out with the wrong power of ten, do not re-read the derivation: write the formula three times with different numbers and check it against the textbook's solved example.

Finish by putting a small date next to each line you wrote. That date is the only part of this method that needs willpower later.

04Close the book and say it out loud

Shut the book and explain the chapter to the wall, a sibling, or the empty chair, in about two minutes. If you cannot explain why a step follows from the one before, you found the next C.

Talking has one advantage over writing: it is too fast to hide behind neat handwriting. You hear yourself make a jump and you notice it. Where a recording device is available, record it and listen once — the second listen is where you hear the mistake.

05Come back in two days, for six minutes

A day or two later, take out the page you wrote and do the same five-minute recall, on a fresh sheet, without the book. Then compare the two sheets. The second one should be longer for the C items and identical for the A items; if an A item has fallen back to B, it was never as solid as it looked.

Six minutes, two days later, is worth more than a second full read. The gap is doing the work: you forget a little, then you pull it back, and that pull is what makes it stay for the exam rather than for the evening.

06The night before

Do not start a new chapter, and do not re-read everything. Do this instead:

  1. Read your own one-line summaries, out loud, in order. Twenty minutes for a whole subject.
  2. For each line, say one example or one formula without looking.
  3. Write down the three things you could not say, on a separate slip, and put that slip in your bag.
  4. Sleep. A tired mind at midnight is a worse investment than a fresh one at seven.

If you have a stack of chapters to get through, the app can turn your own pages into questions and cards so the recall is not a blank page every time — the quiz and flashcard screens run on the material you add and keep the page number attached. The method above is what matters; the tool only removes the excuses.

How you know it worked

You will know it worked when the second blank page, two days later, is fuller than the first one — without reading the chapter again.
