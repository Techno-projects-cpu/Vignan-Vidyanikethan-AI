02

“It will not answer from nothing. If your page does not cover it, it says so, and only then offers a search, clearly labelled as outside your books.”

Most school AI projects are a wrapper around a chat model: ask anything, get a confident paragraph, check nothing. This one refuses when the evidence is thin.

It is not a newly trained model either. It is an orchestrator: retrieval over your own pages, a citation that has to name a page, a rule that stops the answer when the page is not there, and a local model option for when the cloud is not available.

03

Eleven screens.
One drawer.

The app opens as a chat. Everything else is one tap from the small logo in the corner. No bar, no tabs, nothing to learn first.

01 Chat The whole app, really. Ask, paste a link, drop a chapter, and read the citation under the answer. 02 Library PDFs, readable text, pasted notes, public links. Scanned school books are read with a built-in OCR path. 03 Quiz Multiple choice, fill in the blank, short answer, or mixed, easy to hard, timed or not, written from your own pages. 04 Exam practice JEE Main, NEET, AP EAPCET and JEE Advanced in the real paper's shape. No past papers: the pattern is public, the questions come from your pages. 05 Flashcards Cited decks that come back on a schedule, and a deck built from the questions you got wrong. 06 Code practice Python, JavaScript and HTML & CSS: twelve short lessons, any pasted program explained line by line, and “what will this print?”. Runs with the Wi-Fi off. 07 Progress Streak, points, accuracy, topic mastery, and one honest next step instead of a wall of charts. 08 Friends Study partners by username, with a small monogram instead of a photo. Nobody uploads a face. 09 Study Hall Talk to friends, or to other students using the app, mostly about work. There is a report button and a block list. 10 Play Four small games (chess, a quiz duel, a maths sprint, a word game) for the ten minutes between two chapters. 11 Settings Six styles, thirty-three languages, the offline switch, and one button that deletes everything this device holds.

05

Four steps,
no shortcuts.

The route from a question to a sentence you can point at, in the order it happens.

  1. 01

    Your pages, indexed on your device

    Add a chapter and it is split into passages and indexed in the browser. The books never leave the phone; nothing is uploaded to a server to be answered from.

  2. 02

    Retrieve before writing

    The question is matched against those passages first. A follow-up like “why is it important?” is read against the question before it, so the topic carries instead of resetting.

  3. 03

    Cite, or decline

    If the pages are thin, the answer says so and offers the labelled web search instead. Outside material is always marked as outside, never quietly mixed in.

  4. 04

    You check it by hand

    Every citation names the file and the page. Open the page, find the sentence. If it is not there, that is a bug. The judge page says where to report it.

06

Ten pieces,
about studying.

Not about the product. Each one is a method you can use tonight with a book, a phone and a pen. Ten of them, published 7 November 2026.

01 How to check an AI answer before you trust it Read the citation before the answer, then find the sentence on the page. Four failure modes, one five-minute drill. 02 How to revise a chapter you have already forgotten A blank page, three piles, six minutes two days later. Why re-reading feels like revision and is not. 03 How to study with only a phone One folder, aeroplane mode, one question per paragraph, and the answer written by hand. 04 Why writing your own questions beats re-reading Five questions from one paragraph, the stems that do the work, and the question that would fail you. 05 A fixed routine for physics numericals Most marks are lost in the first line. Given, asked, draw, relation, rearrange, check the size, answer. 06 How to explain a school project in sixty seconds Five sentences a judge can act on, and what to say when you do not know the answer. 07 Why your marks dropped in one chapter and not another Find the exact sentence where you stopped understanding. One sentence is easier to fix than one chapter. 08 How to answer a five-mark question Five marks is five things the examiner ticks, not five sentences. The shape that earns all five, and the line nobody writes. 09 How to prepare for a viva you were not expecting Rehearse being pushed four times, and the sentence to say when you do not know. 10 A demo that survives a power cut Bad Wi-Fi, a flat battery and an eleven-person queue. Test it the way it will fail, and hand the judge the phone.
