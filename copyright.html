THE SMALL PRINT / 04

Copyright
& takedown.

Whose words are in the app, why no textbook is bundled, and how a rights holder asks for something to be removed.

Last updated 29 October 2026

01 / No textbook is bundled or hosted

Vignan Vidyanikethan AI ships with no textbook, no chapter text and no scanned page. NCERT and SCERT publications are copyrighted and their terms prohibit republication, storing them in a retrieval system, or offering them from another service without written permission. This project therefore neither bundles nor indexes them, and it does not mirror their PDFs. What you see is either text you loaded yourself or a short quotation from a page you provided.

Students can add material they have permission to use — their own notes, a chapter their school allows them to digitise, a public-domain text, or a page they wrote. Uploading is a local action: the file is extracted in your browser and stays there unless you choose a feature that sends part of it to an AI or search provider.

02 / What the app does with a page

The app quotes at most a few sentences from the pages you supplied so an answer can point back to the page it came from, with the page number attached. Quotations are shown inside your own session and are attributed to your own file. They are not published, indexed for strangers, or turned into a downloadable copy of the book. Model-paper previews and study sheets are assembled from the same local pages and stay on your device unless you export or print them yourself.

03 / Outside search results

When web fallback runs, the app reads a small number of public pages and quotes short excerpts, always labelled as an outside source rather than a textbook, with the source name and address shown. It is not intended to reproduce long passages from a website, and the summary is written for a student, not republished as a copy of the article. If you are a publisher and would prefer your site not to be read this way, see the takedown route below.

04 / The project's own work

The name, interface, code and writing of this project belong to its student author and the school project it is part of. Please ask before reusing the branding or presenting the app as your own. Open-source libraries used here keep their own licences, listed in OPEN_SOURCE_CREDITS.md, and the Nezaad and Zero Vast marks belong to their owners.

05 / Reporting a copyright problem

If you believe something in this deployment infringes your rights, contact the project operator — the student, the teacher in charge, or the school office of Vignan Vidyanikethan English Medium High School — and include: the work you own, the exact page or answer where it appears, the URL, your contact details, and a statement that you are the rights holder or authorised to act for them. A precise report can be acted on quickly; vague notices usually cannot. Machine-readable contact details are published at /.well-known/security.txt.

Files a student loaded into their own browser are not visible to the operator and cannot be removed remotely. In that case we can explain how the student deletes local data, and can adjust the guidance the app gives about what may be uploaded. Where a takedown concerns something published by the project itself, the operator will review it, remove or replace the material if the claim is valid, and reply to the reporter.

06 / Fair use, fair dealing and study

Quoting a sentence to explain it to the student who supplied the page is ordinary study use. That does not extend to redistributing a book, publishing answer keys copied from a publisher's guide, or assembling a substitute for a textbook. The app is deliberately built so that it cannot become a library of copyrighted books: nothing is bundled, nothing is shared between users, and every quotation is tied to a page the student already has.

07 / Corrections

If an attribution, page number or quotation is wrong, that is a bug worth fixing. Report it with the page and the answer, and the operator will correct the attribution or remove the quotation. Accuracy matters here precisely because every answer is meant to be checkable against the book it came from.
