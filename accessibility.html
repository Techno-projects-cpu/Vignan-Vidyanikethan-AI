Accessibility
& known gaps.

Aimed at WCAG 2.2 level AA, measured where it can be measured, and honest about the parts a student build has not reached yet.

Last updated 27 October 2026

01 / What is aimed for

This site targets WCAG 2.2 level AA. It is not certified, and no third-party audit has been done. The commitment that can be checked is narrower and firmer: text contrast, keyboard reachability, focus visibility, motion control, zoom and screen-reader names are tested in this repository's own test suite, so a regression fails the build before it reaches you.

02 / Contrast is measured, not judged

Selected theme palette tokens are checked against a 4.5:1 floor — including text that sits over a painted background behind a glass panel, which is the hardest case. The suite composites the panel tint over each colour in the artwork and fails if the resulting ink ratio drops below the floor. These automated palette checks are not a complete rendered contrast audit of every screen or glass background; the painted themes were re-derived twice for exactly this reason.

03 / Keyboard and focus

The interface aims to make clickable controls reachable with Tab and activated with Enter or Space; the composer sends with Enter and breaks lines with Shift+Enter; Esc closes the open drawer, dialog or menu and returns focus to where it was. Focus styles and skip links are implemented, but complete keyboard and assistive-technology coverage has not been independently audited.

04 / Motion

Animations are decorative, never required to read anything, and never carry words on their own. When the operating system asks for reduced motion, the drifting backgrounds, the ink-wash reveals, the glass sweep and the panel stagger are all switched off, and the page is presented in its final state. No animation runs longer than it needs to, and nothing flashes more than three times a second.

05 / Type, zoom and colour

Text remains readable at 200% zoom and with the browser's own font-size override; the layout reflows to one column on phones. The interface never uses colour alone to carry meaning — a wrong answer is marked with a word and a shape as well as a colour. Appearance settings include theme choices alongside a dark/light choice, and the whole palette can be switched to plain ink-on-paper if the painted themes are hard to read.

06 / Known gaps

These are real and are listed rather than hidden. The hand-painted backgrounds are decorative but can be visually busy for some readers; the plain theme exists for that. Canvas-based elements such as the sound-reactive study view have limited screen-reader output. Chat streaming announces new text through a polite live region, which some screen readers read unevenly. The app offers 33 interface languages, but legal documents and some help text remain English; translation quality and screen-reader behaviour have not been fully audited in every language. The board and chat games are usable by keyboard but their timing is not adjustable. Reports of anything else are welcome and will be added here.

07 / Telling us

Accessibility problems count as bugs. Send them to the project operator (the student, the teacher in charge, or the school office) with the page, what you used, and what got in the way. If a teacher or a student needs a specific adjustment for class use — larger type, higher contrast, no motion by default — say so; a request from the school is treated as a requirement, not a preference.
